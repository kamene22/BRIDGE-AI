# Higher Education Loans Board (HELB) Repayment and Compliance Guide

*Authoritative legal and operational guide on HELB student loan repayment procedures, loanee compliance obligations, grace periods, penalties, and employer statutory duties under the Higher Education Loans Board Act.*

---

## 1. Statutory Framework and Legal Authority

The Higher Education Loans Board (HELB) is a statutory body established under the **Higher Education Loans Board Act (Cap. 213A, Laws of Kenya)**. HELB is mandated to grant loans, bursaries, and scholarships to Kenyan students pursuing higher education in recognized universities and tertiary institutions.

- **Legal Authority:** Higher Education Loans Board Act (Cap. 213A).
- **Compliance Binding Effect:** Every beneficiary of a HELB loan is legally obligated under Section 15 of the HELB Act to repay their loan upon completion of higher education or upon gainful employment.

---

## 2. Loanee Repayment Obligations & Grace Period

### 1. The One-Year Grace Period
- **Rule:** Under Section 15(1) of the HELB Act, a loan beneficiary is granted a statutory **grace period of one (1) year** immediately following completion of their degree or diploma program before loan repayment becomes mandatory.
- **Immediate Repayment Condition:** If a beneficiary secures gainful employment or income-generating activities before the expiration of the one-year grace period, their obligation to begin HELB loan repayment commences immediately upon receiving their first earnings.

### 2. Mandatory Notification Duty
- Every loan beneficiary has a legal duty to inform HELB of their contact address, employment status, employer name, and physical location within **one month** of completing their studies or securing employment.

---

## 3. Repayment Channels & Monthly Deductions

### 1. Employer Statutory Payroll Deductions (Section 16)
- **Employer Duty:** Every employer in Kenya who hires a HELB loan beneficiary is required by law to notify HELB within **30 days** of employment and deduct loan repayments directly from the employee's monthly salary.
- **Remittance Deadline:** Employers must remit deducted HELB payments to the Board on or before the **15th day** of the following month.

### 2. Self-Employed & Individual Electronic Payments
- Beneficiaries who are self-employed, freelancing, or making voluntary extra payments can remit funds via:
  - **M-Pesa Paybill:** Business Number `200800`, Account Number: `Loanee National ID Number`.
  - **HELB Customer Self-Service Portal / Mobile App:** Online portal repayment using Debit Card, M-Pesa, or direct bank transfer.
  - **Bank Direct Deposit:** Designated HELB collection accounts at KCB, Equity Bank, Co-operative Bank, or Absa Bank.

---

## 4. Default Penalties and Compliance Certificates

### 1. Default Penalty Rates
- **Monthly Non-Repayment Fine:** Under Section 15(2) of the Act, if a loan beneficiary defaults on repayment after the grace period without notifying the Board, a statutory penalty of **KES 500 per month** is levied on the unpaid loan balance for every month the loan remains in default.
- **Employer Non-Remittance Penalty:** Any employer who fails to deduct or remit HELB funds from an employee's salary is liable to a penalty equal to **5% of the total amount due** for each month of default.

### 2. HELB Compliance Certificate (Clearance Certificate)
- A **HELB Compliance Certificate** (or HELB Clearance Certificate for non-beneficiaries) is an official document issued by HELB confirming that an individual has cleared their student loan or is actively servicing their loan in good standing.
- **Job Application Requirement:** Many Kenyan public bodies, NGOs, and corporate employers require job applicants to submit a valid HELB Compliance Certificate as part of Chapter 6 Integrity clearance documents.
